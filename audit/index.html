The short version

Your site loads fast and has the right pages, but it's a 2023 template that was never made yours. It tries to talk to buyers, sellers, investors, agents and contractors all at once, so a homeowner who needs to sell doesn't see a clear reason to call you. A few problems also cost you trust and leads every day.

Urgent: fix these first

Trust

The testimonials are the template's samples

"John D.", "Jim D." and "Kristen W." with general praise come with the website template. Your Reviews page and Current Projects page are also empty. A homeowner deciding whether to trust you with their house will notice.

Show your real Google reviews automatically, plus before-and-after photos of projects you've completed.

Trust

Template leftovers make it look unfinished

The header still carries the template's default tagline, "Just another SiteBuilder site", in the code Google reads. The homepage video uses a stock presenter, not you, and the photos are stock images of mainland houses.

Put you, your Oahu projects and your local story front and center.

Worth a quick check

The site says two different things about what you do

The footer says "We are not a Real Estate Broker," but the homepage offers to "price your home competitively, market it effectively, and negotiate the best possible terms," which reads like a listing agent. The About page also lists Credit Repair and Debt Removal. Since those services are regulated, it's worth a quick look with your licensed partner or attorney.

Say clearly what you do: you buy houses directly, as-is, and refer sellers to a licensed agent when listing is better for them.

Costing you leads

Message

The headline could belong to any company

"We Help You Realize Your Real Estate Goals" doesn't tell a homeowner what you do, where, or why to call you instead of anyone else.

"Sell your Oahu house as-is. A fair cash offer in 48 hours."

Focus

Six audiences compete for attention

Buyers, sellers, investors, agents, contractors and wholesale content all get equal space, so no one feels the site was built for them.

Lead with homeowners who need to sell. Give investors their own page, and move agents and contractors to the footer.

Forms

The forms ask for too much

The buyer form asks for a full address and makes Hawaii visitors scroll a 50-state list. The homepage form asks only for an address, with no name or phone, so you can't follow up with people who stop there.

A 60-second, 4-step form: address, condition, timeline, then name and phone, with text-message consent.

Measurement

No way to know what's working

There's no Google Analytics, Facebook pixel or call tracking installed, so there's no record of how many people visit, where they come from, or which pages bring calls.

Install tracking on day one and send you a simple monthly report.

Google search

Google has little to work with

Pages have no search descriptions, the main page heading is literally "Home," and the blog hasn't had a new post since March 2023.

Local pages for each situation and area (inherited houses, foreclosure, Ewa, Kapolei, Waipahu) that Google can match to real searches.

What's already working

  • The site loads quickly.
  • It works well on a phone, and your number is in the header.
  • You already have investor and contractor sign-up pages.
  • Your hibiscus logo and Oahu focus give you a real local identity.
  • Your About page shows BBB Accreditation with an A+ rating since 2019. That's strong proof the homepage doesn't use yet.
